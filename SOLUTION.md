## The true timeline

**Sep 2024** — Noah incorporates **Brightwater Contracting Ltd** (company no.
14882073). He is named nowhere in it. Sole officer: **Janet Whitlock**, his
mother, at 12 Ashfield Terrace, Dunmore. He rents **Unit 14** at a self-storage
yard four streets away in the company's name and puts it on the invoices as the
supplier's delivery address.

**Oct 2024 – Feb 2026** — Kestrel pays Brightwater **£184,600** across **11
invoices**. Three jobs are billed twice, to the penny. Every invoice is approved
by **N. Reid alone**, in breach of the two-signature rule that applies over
£10,000. No VAT number on any of them. Procurement flags the supplier record as
incomplete twice and gets no reply.

**Mon 2 Mar 2026** — Maya, reconciling the Q3 pack, finds the duplicates.

**Tue 3 Mar, 10:12** — She requests the supplier file from Procurement. The
Supplier Transparency policy auto-copies it to every project lead. *This is how
Noah learns she is looking* — twelve days before she disappears.

**Wed 4 Mar, 09:04** — Half an hour before the working day, she searches
Companies House. One officer, Janet Whitlock, one appointment. Street View shows
the registered office is a house.

**Thu 5 Mar, 19:44** — She builds a private workbook and password-protects it.

**Thu 12 Mar, 17:52** — She drives to Dunmore and photographs Unit 14 from her
car. Noah's grey estate, **KP63 HWD**, is at the shutter with the boot open. She
moves the photo to her hidden album three minutes later — the only time she does
that all year.

**Thu 12 Mar, 18:20** — Noah messages: *"You drove out to Dunmore yesterday."*
He has the day wrong, but he knows.

**Fri 13 Mar, 16:42** — She emails Compliance asking for 9am Monday, off-site,
about "a supplier account… somebody internal". She will bring hard copies.

**Fri 13 Mar, 18:05** — Ninety minutes later Noah emails her about Brightwater
and tells her to come to him first. He explains in the email itself how he knows.

**Sat 14 Mar, 20:40** — Maya arrives at 38 Calder Row. Noah is at the edge of a
22:18 photograph in a dark green waxed jacket. He will tell police he arrived at
21:50 and left at 22:40; no guest can place him doing either.

**22:31** — Noah: *"Can we talk. Five minutes. Not in here."* She refuses. *"It
has to be tonight."*

**23:47** — Tara Nolan, outside for a cigarette, hears Maya arguing furiously
with a man by the bins. Too dark and wet to see who.

**23:52** — Doorbell camera: Maya leaves alone, green jacket, turns right toward
Marlow Street. **Last confirmed sighting.**

**23:58** — A guest photographs the street. A dark grey estate pulls off the
Marlow Street corner, indicator on, plate legible as **KP63 … H**. Noah's
statement puts him at home for the past hour and eighteen minutes.

**00:03 / 00:11 / 00:22** — He calls (72s). She calls back (41s). He calls once
more for **6 seconds** — long enough to say he's outside. Cell site has her on
the Calder Row mast for the first two and moving east by the third. She gets in
because he offers to show her the paperwork.

**00:29** — Zoe rings. Unanswered.

**00:38** — Handset registers on **DUN-04, Dunmore Industrial Estate** —
16 minutes east, the opposite direction from her home. **Noah kills her at Unit
14**, where the paperwork is stored, where the CCTV covers only the gate and
there is no staff on site after six.

**00:47 — THE LAST MESSAGE.** From inside that cell he sends, from her phone, to
Zoe: *"Heading home. Don't wait up x"*. It buys him the night, and it is what
undoes him.

**01:29 – 01:44** — He drives to Sefton Bridge, a place she walked often, and
leaves her jacket and phone on the towpath. Three minutes of registration, then
nothing. **Neither item is wet through** after six hours of rain, because both
had been in a car and the rain had stopped.

**Wed 18 Mar, 08:47** — Noah emails Compliance offering to take over the
supplier reconciliation, "since I know the Brightwater account better than
anyone here." The only true sentence in the email.

## The five independent chains

Each of these points at Noah on its own. Together they are conclusive.

1. **The locked workbook** (`file-reconciliation`) — 11 invoices, £184,600,
   every one approved by N. Reid alone against a two-signature rule, three
   billed twice. And the delivery address of a groundworks contractor is a
   storage unit with no vehicle access. Money, man, place.
2. **Companies House + the bake sale** (`web-companies-house` +
   `mail-bakesale`) — the register gives Brightwater's sole officer as Janet
   Whitlock, which means nothing on its own. The most boring email in the
   mailbox — *"special mention to Janet Whitlock, Noah's mum, who drove over
   from Dunmore"* — gives the shell company an owner.
3. **The plate** (`photo-car-park` + `photo-street`) — a throwaway shot of
   someone parking across two bays is the only thing tying **KP63 HWD** to
   N. Reid by name, in a bay stencilled with it. Then the 23:58 street photo
   catches that plate leaving Calder Row six minutes after Maya walked out —
   against a statement placing him at home since 22:40.
4. **The call log** (`calls-night` + `calls-after`) — three calls in nineteen
   minutes ending in a six-second "I'm outside". Then read the days after: Zoe
   rings six times, Erin twice, her mother three times, Tara once. **Noah never
   rings again.** He is the only person in her life who never tries to find her.
5. **The last message + cell site** (`msg-zoe` + `file-cellsite`) — the 00:47
   message opens with a capital and contains an apostrophe. Maya does neither,
   in any thread, in any month, across 42 outgoing messages: "dont", "im",
   "cant", "thats", "shes". And the cell site puts that message on an industrial
   estate seven miles off any route home, with the phone sitting there for a
   further 25 minutes.

The unlock chain: the **email from Sana** ("our first flat, the flat number, the
year we moved in") plus **IMG_0417** ("ASHCOMBE HOUSE — FLAT 4B", dated
2019-09-21) gives `ashcombe4b2019`. Opening the workbook surfaces the Unit 14
address, which unlocks the **Dunmore map**, the **cell site analysis** and
**IMG_2288** — the photo Maya took of Unit 14 three days before she died.

## The red herrings, and why each dissolves

1. **Liam Cross, the ex.** Nine calls and six messages in one evening, a
   voicemail at 22:44 admitting he was twenty feet from the door, two guests
   placing him on Calder Row at 23:20, a £2,000 payment from Maya, and her own
   searches for non-molestation orders. An annotated bank statement recommends
   him for early interview.
   *Actually:* he was there for **Zoe** — they'd been seeing each other three
   weeks. His contact stops dead at 22:58 and never resumes; not one call in the
   four days his ex-girlfriend was missing, because from 23:20 he was with Zoe
   and neither wanted to explain why. The £2,000 was Maya repaying her half of a
   holiday deposit, and he thanks her for it in a message she kept. The
   annotation on the statement is by an unnamed reviewer and is corroborated by
   nothing.
2. **Zoe Bennett, the best friend.** Four inbound messages deleted from Maya's
   phone between 23:05 and 23:38. A statement that she was "in the kitchen
   basically the whole night", contradicted by two guests. And she received the
   last message.
   *Actually:* the deleted messages were her arranging to get outside to Liam.
   She lied for the ordinary reason people lie — to hide an embarrassment — and
   then couldn't take it back. Her undelivered message on 16 March is a
   confession to being a bad friend, not to anything else.
3. **Erin Vale, the host.** Said the party ended at 00:30; a photo from her front
   room is timestamped 01:20 with her awake in it. She destroyed her guest list
   and emailed four people telling them to keep quiet, adding "I know how this
   looks."
   *Actually:* the blue tin on her kitchen shelf at 21:34 and gone by 01:20 was
   neither hers nor legal. She moved the end time and deleted the list to keep
   police out of her kitchen. Everything she lied about is true and none of it
   is about Maya.
4. **The runaway.** One-way Lisbon flights at 23:11 on 8 March, jobs in
   Portugal, £600 cash out three days before, and a to-do list ending "if this
   goes badly on monday, start looking. lisbon? anywhere."
   *Actually:* she planned to leave the job and the city **after** reporting the
   fraud, not instead of it — which is what the whistleblowing searches on the
   same evening are about. The £600 was a holding deposit on a flat, receipted
   in branch, with a viewing booked for 19 March.
5. **The canal.** Jacket and phone found together on the Sefton Bridge towpath,
   a place she walked often and had photographed a fortnight earlier. The search
   went into the water.
   *Actually:* they were placed. Both were dry after six hours of rain on an
   open towpath — the investigator's own note says so, and it is the most
   overlooked line in the file. Sefton Bridge is nine minutes by road from
   Dunmore and in exactly the wrong direction from everything else.
